Correct AI Usage

Our Paragraph:
Students should use AI as a study partner, not as a replacement for their own thinking.
It is fine to ask AI to explain a hard concept, quiz you before an exam, or check your grammar.
Any AI help should be cited and allowed by the instructor's rules for that assignment.
The final work you turn in should always show your own ideas and understanding.

AI Response (Critique):
Your reasoning is solid: treating AI as a tutor keeps the learning with you.
One alternative view is that "allowed" differs from class to class, so students should check each syllabus instead of assuming one rule fits all.
It also helps to verify AI answers against course materials, because AI can sound confident while being wrong.
Overall, citing AI and keeping your own voice in the final work is a strong, honest approach.

Incorrect AI Usage

Three Incorrect Ways to Use AI

1. Plagiarism
Using AI to generate content and presenting it as your own work without proper attribution is considered plagiarism. This can lead to academic or professional consequences.

2. Misinformation
Using AI to spread false or misleading information can have serious consequences, especially in areas like health, politics, and news.

3. Bias and Discrimination
AI systems can perpetuate and amplify existing biases if not properly designed and monitored, leading to unfair treatment of certain groups.

Artificial Intelligence can be an extremely helpful tool, but students must use it responsibly. Submitting AI-generated work without permission can be dishonest. Students should always check information and follow their instructors' policies. In my opinion, AI should help students learn rather than replacing their own thinking.
