Malware and Cyber Threats

The Historic WannaCry Ransomware Attack

A massive worldwide cyberattack on May 12, 2017 was unleashed and saw the WannaCry cryptoworm spread through the network of connected Windows computers around the globe. Self-propagating ransomware, exploiting an unpatched network vulnerability called EternalBlue, encrypted victims' files automatically and demanded Bitcoin ransom payments without anyone needing to click a phishing link. In a matter of hours, the outbreak disabled more than 200,000 computers in more than 150 countries, forcing hospitals in the National Health Service in the United Kingdom to divert ambulances and causing billions of dollars in global economic losses. WannaCry remains a landmark event in cybersecurity history because it proved how rapidly unpatched legacy systems can paralyze critical infrastructure and forced organizations worldwide to prioritize routine software patching.

Types of Malware

  1. Ransomware: Malicious software that encrypts a user's files and demands payment to restore access.
  2. Trojans: Deceptive programs disguised as legitimate software that secretly open backdoor access for attackers.

Common Hacker Motives

  1. Financial Gain: Extorting organizations through ransomware or stealing banking and customer data for profit.
  2. Political Activism: Disrupting government or corporate operations to promote a political or social agenda.

Prevention Strategies

  • Multi-Factor Authentication: Requiring two or more verification factors before granting account access.
  • Firewalls: Monitoring and filtering incoming and outgoing network traffic to block unauthorized connections.
