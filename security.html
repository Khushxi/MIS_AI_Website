The Historic WannaCry Ransomware Attack

On May 12, 2017, a massive global cyberattack unleashed the WannaCry cryptoworm across interconnected Windows computer networks worldwide. By exploiting an unpatched network vulnerability known as EternalBlue, this self-propagating ransomware automatically encrypted victims' files and demanded ransom payments in Bitcoin without requiring anyone to click a phishing link. Within hours, the outbreak crippled more than 200,000 computers across 150 countries, forcing hospitals in the United Kingdom's National Health Service to divert ambulances and causing billions of dollars in global economic losses. WannaCry remains a landmark event in cybersecurity history because it proved how rapidly unpatched legacy systems can paralyze critical infrastructure and forced organizations worldwide to prioritize routine software patching.

Types of Malware

  1. Ransomware: Malicious software that encrypts a user's files and demands payment to restore access.
  2. Trojans: Deceptive programs disguised as legitimate software that secretly open backdoor access for attackers.

Common Hacker Motives

  1. Financial Gain: Extorting organizations through ransomware or stealing banking and customer data for profit.
  2. Political Activism: Disrupting government or corporate operations to promote a political or social agenda.

Prevention Strategies

  • Multi-Factor Authentication: Requiring two or more verification factors before granting account access.
  • Firewalls: Monitoring and filtering incoming and outgoing network traffic to block unauthorized connections.
